
Master Scriptwriting Guidelines

⚬ Format & Language:
  ⚬ Structure all scripts in a 9:16 vertical reel format.
  ⚬ Write all visual, camera, and setting directions in English.
  ⚬ Write all spoken dialogue in natural Hindustani using Devanagari script.
⚬ Scene Scope & Duration:
  ⚬ Determine the scene count dynamically based on the narrative arc, story scope, or specific user request.
  ⚬ Cap each individual scene at a maximum of 10 seconds (minimum duration flexible).
  ⚬ State the exact scene runtime directly in the heading: ### SCENE X: Title [X Seconds].
⚬ Timestamp Scoping & Precision:
  ⚬ Reset audio, dialogue, and SFX timestamps to [0:00] at the start of each scene.
  ⚬ Attach explicit start-to-end timestamps [0:XX – 0:XX] directly to every spoken dialogue line and sound effect cue.
⚬ Shot Structure:
  ⚬ Use clean, plain numbered headings without timestamps: #### Shot 1, #### Shot 2.
⚬ Performance & Delivery Direction:
  ⚬ Format every spoken line as `CHARACTER [Tone] [0:XX – 0:XX]: "Dialogue"`, pacing speech at roughly 2 to 3 words per second (urgent/fast tones near 3 wps, calm/sarcastic tones near 2 wps) relative to the scene runtime without rigid word limits.
  ⚬ Example:
    ⚬ RAMESHWAR [Frantic] [0:01 – 0:04.5]:
      "अरे भगाने गए तो हाथ में काट लिया, और खुद पूरी बोतल गटक गई!"
    ⚬ SUNITA [Sarcastic] [0:05 – 0:09]:
      "बिना चखने के पूरी बोतल साफ, अब वन विभाग ही संभाले!"
⚬ Seamless Scene-to-Scene Connectivity:
  ⚬ Direct Consequence: Open subsequent scenes with immediate reactions to the outcome of the previous scene.
  ⚬ Visual Anchors: Keep primary subjects, key environmental focal points, or focal character states from the earlier scene visible in the background or adjacent space across cuts.
  ⚬ Audio Bleed: Carry over prominent trailing sound effects or background ambiances from the end of one scene into the opening 1–2 seconds of the following scene to bridge transitions.
  ⚬ Conversational Carryover: Have the opening dialogue of a new scene directly reference the specific event that just occurred.
⚬ Spatial Consistency & Cast Isolation:
  ⚬ Maintain a unified, shared physical setting viewed from adjacent perspectives, reverse angles, or spatial thresholds.
  ⚬ Rotate the on-screen cast so that characters featured in one scene do not appear in the subsequent scene.
⚬ Minimal Prop Execution:
  ⚬ Rely on expressive facial reactions, exaggerated gestures, dynamic vocal delivery, and environmental staging to drive the narrative.
  ⚬ Keep physical props minimal, simple, and stationary within the environment to ensure reliable visual generation.
